I. EMERGENCY DRUGS

A

PRIMARY (ESSENTIAL) EMERGENCY DRUGS

Adrenaline / Epinephrine (1:1000, 1 mg/mL)

Indication: Severe anaphylaxis, acute asthma, cardiac arrest
Adult Dose: 0.3–0.5 mg (0.3–0.5 mL) IM/SC; q5–15m prn
Pediatric Dose: 0.01 mg/kg (0.01 mL/kg) IM (max 0.3 mg)

Oxygen (100%)

Indication: Hypoxia, syncope, acute angina, dyspnea, bacard respiratory distress
Dose: 6–10 L/min via face mask or BVM

Nitroglycerin

Indication: Acute anginal pain / ischemic chest pain (contraindicated if BP <90 mmHg/PDE-5)
Dose: 0.4 mg SL (tablet/spray); repeat q5m (max 3 doses/15m)

Injectable Antihistamine (Diphenhydramine / Chlorpheniramine)

Indication: Delayed allergic reactions, mild skin reactions (urticaria, pruritus)
Dose: Diphenhydramine: 25–50 mg IM/slow IV | Chlorpheniramine: 10–20 mg IM/slow IV

Injectable Corticosteroid (Hydrocortisone / Dexamethasone)

Indication: Adjunctive in severe anaphylaxis, acute adrenal insufficiency, severe asthma
Dose: Hydrocortisone Succinate: 100–200 mg IV/IM | Dexamethasone: 4–8 mg IV/IM

Bronchodilator (Salbutamol / Albuterol)

Indication: Acute bronchospasm, asthmatic attack
Dose: 2 puffs (90–100 mcg/puff) via MDI; repeat as needed

Oral Carbohydrates / Oral Glucose

Indication: Acute hypoglycemia in a conscious patient
Dose: 15–20 g fast-acting glucose (gel, fruit juice, table sugar)

Aspirin (Aspirin 160–325 mg)

Indication: Suspected acute myocardial infarction (AMI)
Dose: 160–325 mg chewed and swallowed immediately
B

SECONDARY (ADVANCED) EMERGENCY DRUGS

Atropine Sulfate

Indication: Bradycardia, organophosphate poisoning, syncope, hypotension
Dose: 0.5–1.0 mg IV/IM; repeat q3–5m (max 3 mg)

Anticonvulsant (Diazepam / Midazolam)

Indication: Prolonged tonic-clonic seizures, status epilepticus (>5m)
Dose: Diazepam: 5–10 mg slow IV/rectal | Midazolam: 5 mg IM or 2.5–5 mg slow IV

Flumazenil

Indication: Benzodiazepine overdose (respiratory depression, sedation)
Dose: 0.2 mg IV over 30s; 0.1 mg/min (max 1 mg)

50% Dextrose (IV)

Indication: Severe hypoglycemia in an unconscious patient
Dose: 25–50 mL D50W IV push over 2–3 min

II. ANALGESIC (NSAIDS)

A

Centrally Acting Analgesics (Narcotic / Opioid Analgesics)

Tramadol
Dose: 50–100 mg orally q4–6h (Max: 400 mg/day)
Frequency used for: Moderate-to-severe post-surgical pain.
Morphine
Dose: 10 mg IM/SC or 2.5–5 mg IV q4h prn. Reserved for severe post-op pain or trauma cases.
Codeine
Dose: 30–60 mg orally q4–6h (often combined with Paracetamol/Acetaminophen).
Pentazocine
Dose: 50–100 mg IM or orally q3–4h.
B

Peripherally Acting Analgesics (Non-Opioid / NSAIDs)

1

Non-Selective COX Inhibitors

a. Paracetamol (Acetaminophen): 500–650 mg orally q4–6h
b. Ibuprofen: 400–600 mg orally q6–8h after food
c. Ketorolac: 10 mg orally q6h, or 15–30 mg IV/IM q6h
d. Diclofenac Sodium / Potassium: 50 mg orally BD or 75 mg deep IM
e. Aceclofenac: 100 mg orally BD
f. Piroxicam: 20 mg orally OD
g. Mefenamic Acid: 250–500 mg orally TDS
2

Preferential / Selective COX-2 Inhibitors

a. Nimesulide: 100 mg orally BD
b. Celecoxib: 100–200 mg orally BD
c. Etoricoxib: 60–90 mg or 120 mg acute post-surgical pain OD

III. ANTIBIOTICS

Penicillins (Penicillin G and Penicillin V)
Dose: 250–500 mg every 8 hour
Indication: Streptococcal pharyngitis, syphilis, rheumatic fever prophylaxis, pneumococcal infections.
Amoxicillin + Clavulanic Acid
Dose: 625 mg thrice daily
Indication: Lower Respiratory Tract Infections, Sinusitis, Acute otitis media, Urinary Tract Infections, cellulitis
Metronidazole
Dose: 400 mg every 8 hourly
Indication: Bacterial vaginosis, periapical abscess, Intra-abdominal infections, PID, Trichomoniasis, giardiasis, amoebiasis
Macrolides
Dose: 200–400 mg
Indication: Mycoplasma pneumoniae, Whooping cough, Upper & lower respiratory tract infections
Lincosamide
Dose: 150–300 mg every 6 hourly
Indication: cellulitis, wound infection, periapical abscesses, toxic shock syndrome, necrotizing fasciitis
